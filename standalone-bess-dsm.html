How standalone BESS DSM is calculated

DSM settles the difference between what the battery was scheduled to do and what it actually did in each 15-minute block. Discharging is positive MWh and charging is negative MWh.

Deviation is actual minus scheduled MWh. If they match, the DSM amount is zero.

Volume limit is 10% of the scheduled energy, capped at 25 MWh per block by default (equivalent to 100 MW for 15 minutes). The single-block calculator lets you change that cap. For example, if the schedule is 40 MWh and the battery delivers 34 MWh, the shortfall is 6 MWh. The first 4 MWh uses the inside-limit rate and the remaining 2 MWh uses the beyond-limit rate.

RR means reference rate. In CSV analysis, the calculator uses the Wt. Avg. Hybrid Rate when it is positive and falls back to Normal Rate. The file may use a different rate, especially for charging blocks.

The tables show the percentage of RR applied to each portion of the deviation. For example, at an RR of ₹10/kWh, 150% equals ₹15/kWh for that portion.

Reference Rate (RR) and Normal Rate (NR)

ComparisonReference Rate (RR)Normal Rate (NR)
What sets it?The applicable tariff or transaction price. When multiple rates apply, a weighted average may be used.Under CERC’s 2024 Regulation 7(1), take the highest of the weighted day-ahead price, the weighted real-time price, and the average of those two prices and the ancillary service charge.
Simple exampleIf your weighted transaction rate is ₹8/kWh, the RR is ₹8/kWh.If day-ahead, real-time, and ancillary service rates are ₹5, ₹7, and ₹9/kWh, their average is ₹7/kWh. The highest of ₹5, ₹7, and ₹7 is ₹7/kWh, so that is the NR.

In CSV analysis, the calculator uses a positive Wt. Avg. Hybrid Rate as its base rate and falls back to Normal Rate otherwise. Grid frequency determines the multiplier applied to the base rate.
